WHICH FLOWS HAVE PROVABLE BASELINE PARITY, AND WHICH CANNOT
===========================================================

Why this file exists
--------------------
migrated/comparison.txt reports "match" or "DIFFER" per flow, and a reader can take a page of "match"
rows as a page of demonstrated equivalence. For four of these eight flows that reading is
wrong, and the distinction is not visible from the word "match" alone. Two captures that both
failed to exercise a flow agree perfectly about having observed nothing. That is agreement
WITHOUT EVIDENCE, and it is a different thing from agreement with evidence.

This file separates the two, so that no downstream summary can convert the second into the
first. It asserts nothing new: every classification below is read off the committed .status
files and migrated/comparison.txt.

Tier 1 — parity DEMONSTRATED, both captures observed the behaviour and agreed
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  flow-2-views              .status 19 records agree, .out 153 projected records agree
  flow-4-solr-search        .status  8 records agree, .out  26 projected records agree
  flow-6-generated-number   .status  4 records agree, .out  38 projected records agree

  These three reached the application, exercised the path and recorded observations that the
  cross-capture comparison then matched record for record. Their verdicts are
  OBSERVED-INCOMPLETE-n-REQUIREMENTS-UNMET rather than a clean pass, and that qualifier is
  load-bearing: what is demonstrated is that the two runtimes behave the SAME, including in
  the requirements they both leave unmet. Parity is proven; completeness is not claimed.

  flow-6 is the one that matters most for the runtime migration, because generated numbering
  runs through the 43 live .xlsx decision tables and therefore through MVEL, the highest-impact
  library change in the backend. Its agreement is evidence that the MVEL advance did not change
  numbering behaviour.

Tier 2 — parity NOT provable, the baseline never exercised the flow
------------------------------------------------------------------
  flow-3-alfresco-roundtrip NOT-EXERCISED-NO-FIXTURE-OBJECT       (both captures)
  flow-5-activemq-event     NOT-EXERCISED-NO-EVENT-PRODUCED       (both captures)
  flow-7-workflow-start     NOT-EXERCISED-WORKFLOW-NOT-STARTED    (both captures)
  flow-8-queue-transition   NOT-EXERCISED-NO-CASE-FILE-AVAILABLE  (both captures)

  For these four, migrated/comparison.txt's "match" means the two captures agree that neither of them
  observed the behaviour. Nothing about Alfresco document round-tripping, ActiveMQ delivery,
  process instantiation or queue transition is demonstrated by this evidence, in either
  direction. Stating that they "match" without stating this would be the single most misleading
  sentence available in this archive.

  The cause is a fixture chain, not a runtime fault, and it is the same chain in all four:
  no case-file object could be created, so there was nothing to attach a document to, nothing
  to generate an event about, nothing to start a workflow on and nothing to move between
  queues. flow-7 additionally records DEFINITIONS_LOADED: 34 against DEFINITIONS_ON_DISK: 36
  and RESIDUAL_RISK: process-engine-observed-here-not-cleared, which is the honest form of the
  Activiti residual risk: the engine loaded definitions, and no process was instantiated.

  This cannot be repaired by re-running the migrated capture. The baseline was taken on JDK 8
  at the base commit and is not reproducible now — the runtime, the deployed build and the
  fixture state have all moved — so a richer migrated capture would have nothing to be
  compared against. Any future closure has to re-establish both halves together.

Tier 3 — the captures DIFFER, and the migrated side is the stronger one
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  flow-1-login  this=OBSERVED-AUTHENTICATED-AND-IDENTITY-CONFIRMED
                other=OBSERVED-INCOMPLETE-1-REQUIREMENTS-UNMET

  The verdict differs while all 5 .status records and all 60 projected .out records agree. The
  migrated capture authenticated and confirmed the identity; the baseline capture did not get
  that far. This is a difference in what the CAPTURE achieved, not in what the application did:
  the underlying records the two share are identical. It is counted as a required difference
  rather than waved through, and it is the one difference in this archive where the migrated
  side observed more rather than less.

The two artifact differences are not in this accounting
------------------------------------------------------
REQUIRED-DIFFERENCES: 3 counts flow-1's verdict plus the two frontend artifact digests,
application.min.js and application.min.css. Those two are a separate subject with their own
record: ../ambiguity-resolutions.md §6a characterises both to the byte and states what the
comparison does and does not establish, and ../pre-existing-defects.md entry 13 registers them as
an accepted deviation. Neither is a flow.

The honest summary, in one line
-------------------------------
Three of eight flows demonstrate baseline parity on observed behaviour; one differs in the
capture's favour with identical underlying records; four are unexercised on both sides and
therefore prove nothing about the integrations they are named for. The four unexercised flows
are exactly the ones covering Alfresco, ActiveMQ, the workflow engine and queue routing, and
their integration behaviour on the target runtime rests on the deployment reaching ready state
with a clean startup log rather than on this archive.
