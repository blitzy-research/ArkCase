why failed and skipped changeSets are mined from the log rather than from
  the changelog table: Liquibase writes a row for a changeSet it EXECUTED
  and writes nothing at all for one that failed or was skipped by a
  precondition.  A table-only account would therefore report those as
  simply absent, which is indistinguishable from never having been
  declared.  The two sources answer different halves of the question and
  both are published.

what this comparison does NOT establish, stated because the boundary
  matters: it establishes what happened to THIS schema on THIS boot.  A
  changeSet carrying failOnError="false" that found nothing to do here
  could still modify a different starting schema, and no before/after
  comparison of one database can rule that out.  That is a property of
  those historical changeSets rather than of this migration, and it is
  registered as a pre-existing condition rather than asserted away.
