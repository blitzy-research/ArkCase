INDEX-READINESS NOTE
    A search index has to have finished indexing before its answer means
    anything.  An empty or short result set from a query issued before indexing
    completed is an artefact of TIMING, not of behaviour, and comparing it
    against the other capture would either manufacture a difference or hide a
    real one behind a matching pair of premature answers.  The query is
    therefore re-issued until two consecutive observations agree on the count,
    at most 12 times at 5s intervals, and a matching pair of ZEROES is not
    accepted as settled - an empty answer to a match-everything query is an
    unpopulated index.  Whether readiness was established is recorded below in
    the RESULT_COUNT_STABLE token; where it could not be established that is
    recorded plainly rather than papered over.
    HOW READINESS WAS OBSERVED, stated because the observation is only as good
    as its method: not by asking the engine whether it had finished, which it
    does not report, but by re-issuing the same match-everything query and
    requiring two consecutive answers to agree on a non-zero count.  The
    outcome is written into the .status file as its own token and read back
    from disk into this record, so this field and that token cannot disagree.
    THE SAME MEANS IS USED IN BOTH RUNS, and that is what makes the two
    readiness observations comparable rather than merely similar: both sides
    are driven by this one unmodified script, so the re-observation test, its
    attempt budget, its interval and its refusal to accept a matching pair of
    zeroes are identical on the pre-migration side and on the replay side by
    construction rather than by convention.  Without this field a low or zero
    match count would be uninterpretable - nothing indexed and ranking changed
    would read the same - and the evidence would collapse back onto an exit
    status, which is exactly what R-T7 forbids.
